### Added (devices and driver requests, 2026-11-09)

- The device core of SPEC-016, behind `CONFIG_EMB_DEVICE` (on by default; off on the tiny profile and AVR): `<emb/device.h>` declares a device as a constant instance with its operations, configuration, RAM state, dependencies and init level. `emb_device_init` and `emb_device_init_list` take each device from UNINIT to READY or FAILED, without calling a driver whose dependency failed; `emb_device_suspend` and `emb_device_resume` refuse a device with requests in flight. Drivers queue requests first in first out with `emb_drv_submit` and end them once with `emb_drv_complete`, from a thread or an interrupt; the caller chooses where the completion goes (polled, a notification bit, a semaphore, a work item or a callback). `emb_drv_cancel` ends a queued request at once and stops a running one through its driver, and `emb_drv_wait` is the blocking wrapper the driver classes build on, cancelling on timeout. Ten conformance cases run on native, QEMU and EmbSIM ([SPEC-016](docs/specs/SPEC-016-device-and-driver-model.md)).
- Devices from the description (SPEC-016 §4, §8): a board's `devices:` names SoC peripherals, or parts by their `compatible`, and `drivers/bindings.yaml` binds each compatible to its driver and fills its configuration from the description. emb-hwgen orders the devices (EARLY first, each after its dependencies, the clock tree before what it gates, ties in the description's order), refuses an unknown compatible, a cycle or an EARLY device on a KERNEL one, and writes `soc_devices.c` and `soc_devices.h`, a Devices table in `board.md`, `derived.devices` in `hw.json`, and in `board.cmake` the sources of only the drivers the devices use. The kernel initializes the EARLY devices in `emb_kernel_init()` and the KERNEL ones in `emb_kernel_start()`; `EMB_DEVICE(name)` reaches a device by symbol ([generator](docs/engineering/hwgen.md)).
- The MPS2 boards' console UART is a device, `uart0`, bound to the new CMSDK UART driver (`drivers/arm/cmsdk_uart.c`), so the QEMU suites initialize a generated device on every Cortex-M run.
- The debug descriptor lists the board's devices (SPEC-016 §9): nine values appended, the version unchanged. `tools/descriptor/descriptor.py` prints them with their class, level, lifecycle and last status, GDB gains `emb devices`, and the GDB check under QEMU requires each to be READY ([debug descriptor](docs/engineering/debug-descriptor.md)).
