# Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W on hardware (2026-11-07)

The first runs of EmbLinkRTOS on an RP2350 and on a Cortex-M33, and the second physical Cortex-M after the [NUCLEO-F446RE](hil-nucleo-2026-11-07.md). The suite ran built by GCC and by Clang, checked and release, at 150 MHz, and every image passed. The bench had no debug probe and no USB-UART adapter, so it ran through the board's USB port and a NUCLEO.

**Board:** Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W (RP2350A), its Arm cores in the Secure state, at 150 MHz from PLL_SYS (12 MHz crystal × 125 / 5 / 2, A102). It executes in place from the 4 MiB QSPI flash, with the boot ROM's quad read setup (EBh, CLKDIV 3, RXDELAY 2).
- **Loading:** picotool 2.3.1 in BOOTSEL (`run_hil.py`, A101). Every test image reboots into BOOTSEL as it ends, so the four suites ran without the button.
- **Console:** UART0 on GPIO0, through a NUCLEO-F446RE running `tools/hil/nucleo-uart-relay`. The relay reads the line on PA10 and forwards it to the ST-LINK's port, `/dev/cu.usbmodem11203`. Its own PA2 header pin is not connected on a stock board.

**Commit:** `7067cf8`, from a clean tree (branch `feat/rp2350-pll`), for all four runs.

## Results

`ctest` on `cm33-pico2w-hil`, each run's records under `hil-runs/`:

| Build | Compiler | Configuration | Passed | Cases | Fault checks that reset the part | Interrupt stack, most used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| checked | Arm GNU Toolchain 14.2.Rel1 (GCC 14.2.1) | `74f22deec579c00f` | 27 of 27 | 167 | 75 | 252 of 2,048 B |
| release | the same | `097edee415c887c6` | 27 of 27 | 165 | none (release) | not measured (release) |
| checked | Homebrew Clang 23.1.5 with LLD 23.1.5 | `74f22deec579c00f` | 27 of 27 | 167 | 75 | 276 of 2,048 B |
| release | the same | `097edee415c887c6` | 27 of 27 | 165 | none (release) | not measured (release) |

What these runs proved on the chip:
- **Fault checks:** every misuse fault check ran. Each one faulted in the kernel, reset the RP2350 through SYSRESETREQ, and was settled when the next boot replayed the case. A reset boots the image again from flash, not BOOTSEL.
- **Crash record:** it survived a real reset (`test_crash_reset`), and held a hardware fault's registers (`test_crash_hw`).
- **Armv8-M stack limits:** they caught an overflowing thread (`test_stack_limit`).
- **FPU:** its state survived preemption (`test_fpu`).

### What 12 MHz showed first

The board's first increment ran from the 12 MHz crystal. There, the GCC checked suite failed four cases:
- three timing checks in `test_sem`, `test_msgq` and `test_thread` were each two ticks late;
- `test_workq` lost a race in its cancel-while-running case.

The same images pass at 150 MHz. The failures came from the slow clock combined with first-time instruction fetches from flash; they were not kernel faults.

## Benchmarks

CPU cycles per operation (DWT CYCCNT, 150 MHz). No FreeRTOS build exists for this board yet, so the NUCLEO-F446RE's release figures sit beside these for scale. The two chips have different cores and memories, so this is not a ranking.

| Operation | Pico 2 W, GCC | Pico 2 W, Clang | NUCLEO-F446RE, GCC |
|---|---|---|---|
| `notify_handover` | 490 | 486 | 794 |
| `sem_handover` | 477 | 456 | 746 |
| `sem_give_take` | 166 | 164 | 194 |
| `isr_wake` | 901 | 850 | 1,514 |

The Cortex-M33 takes about 40% fewer cycles than the Cortex-M4F on every switching path.

`bench_irq`, cycles from the pend to the handler's first instruction, release:

| Build | Average | Minimum | Maximum |
|---|---|---|---|
| GCC | 136 | 132 | 2,417 |
| Clang | 131 | 129 | 2,180 |

The maximum is the first interrupt's: its vector and handler come from flash, and the XIP cache misses on the first fetch. A design that needs a bounded first latency places the handler and its vector in SRAM. The port does not do that yet.

## How to repeat

```sh
tools/hil/nucleo-uart-relay/build.sh --flash   # the NUCLEO becomes the console
cmake --preset cm33-pico2w-hil -DEMB_HIL_PORT=/dev/cu.usbmodem11203
cmake --build --preset cm33-pico2w-hil && ctest --preset cm33-pico2w-hil
```

[The HIL guide](hil.md) shows the wiring: the Pico's GPIO0 to the NUCLEO's PA10 and their grounds together. The first image needs the Pico in BOOTSEL: hold the button while plugging it in. Release builds add `-DEMB_SET=EMB_CHECKED=n`; Clang, `-DCMAKE_TOOLCHAIN_FILE=$PWD/cmake/toolchains/arm-none-eabi-clang.cmake`.
