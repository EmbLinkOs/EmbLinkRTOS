# The flash class on hardware: NUCLEO-F446RE and Pico 2 W (2026-11-10)

This report covers the flash class of SPEC-017 §9 (A122). On each board, the storage partition was erased, programmed and read back, and an erase was timed by the processor's cycle counter against the kernel's clock. Every operation stalls the CPU or holds interrupts off, and the clock caught up afterwards. The whole conformance suite ran on each board with GCC and with Clang, and every image passed. The same STM32 driver runs in CI on EmbSIM's NUCLEO-F446RE, whose flash stalls as the part's does.

**Commit:** `750d3ab`, from a clean tree (branch `feat/m4.2-driver-classes`, PR #140). The class itself is `c4ec917`; `750d3ab` makes the timed erase one of written flash (below).

**Bench:** the wires of the UART, SPI and I2C reports, unchanged; the flash needs none. The partitions, from the boards' descriptions:
- **NUCLEO-F446RE:** `app` is sectors 0 to 6 (384 KiB); `storage` is sector 7, 128 KiB at 0x08060000. It is erased at 32-bit parallelism and programmed a word at a time.
- **Pico 2 W:** `app` is the first 4 MiB less 64 KiB of the W25Q32RV; `storage` is the last sixteen 4 KiB sectors, at 0x103F0000. It goes through the boot ROM's routines, run from RAM with XIP off, a 256-byte page at a time.

`ctest` ran on `cm4-nucleo-hil` and `cm33-pico2w-hil`, checked, with the UART, SPI and I2C loops as before. Each run's records are under `hil-runs/`:

## What the runs show

**The board's flash** (`test_flash_board`):

- **Erased, programmed and read back three times.** Each round erased the whole storage partition, programmed it 256 bytes at a time with a pattern that changes every round, and read every byte back. Each program was also read back by the class itself. A round whose erase had not erased would have failed that read-back with `EMB_EVERIFY`. Last, a page whose bytes are in flash was programmed: on the RP2350 this exercises the copy to RAM before XIP goes off.

- **An erase, timed.** The partition is first programmed all over, then erased. That erase is timed by DWT CYCCNT and by the kernel's clock:

  | Board | Compiler | Erase | By the cycles | By the clock |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | NUCLEO-F446RE | GCC | 128 KiB, one sector | 895 ms | 895 ms |
  | NUCLEO-F446RE | Clang | 128 KiB, one sector | 899 ms | 899 ms |
  | Pico 2 W | GCC | 64 KiB, sixteen sectors | 535 ms | 534 ms |
  | Pico 2 W | Clang | 64 KiB, sixteen sectors | 536 ms | 536 ms |

  Without the catch-up, the NUCLEO's clock would have lost almost the whole 0.9 s. During that time the CPU stalls on every fetch, SysTick's vector included. The Pico's clock would have lost some 33 ms a sector, the time interrupts are held off while XIP is off.

**The class's core** (`test_flash`), on the board as on the host. A NOR flash in RAM, with uneven sectors and a 4-byte write unit, runs the six cases:
- the geometry and sector lookup;
- programs read back, with bits that cannot come back without an erase reported as `EMB_EVERIFY`;
- the refusals, with misuse by reset;
- a partition's bounds;
- a power loss torn into a program and into an erase;
- the polled calls.

## Found on the way

- **An erased sector erases early.** The Pico's first timed erase took some 1.5 ms a sector, because the other case had left the partition erased and the flash finishes erasing a blank sector early. The timed erase now follows a program of the whole partition, and the figures above are of written flash.
- **XIP restored by the boot ROM's own function.** After its flash routines, the RP2350's boot ROM leaves XIP off. pico-sdk 2.1.1 restores it with the XIP setup function the boot ROM left in boot RAM, copied to RAM, and the driver does the same. The QMI's flash timing, which the clock driver set for 150 MHz, and CS1's window are put back after it. The routine, disassembled in both builds, calls only the ROM and that copy, through pointers held in RAM.
