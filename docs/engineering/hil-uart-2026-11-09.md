# The UART class on hardware: NUCLEO-F446RE and Pico 2 W (2026-11-09)

The UART class of SPEC-017 §4 on both of M4's boards: its drivers sending by interrupt and receiving into the class's ring, on the boards' consoles and on a spare UART whose transmit pin is wired to its own receive pin. The whole conformance suite ran on each board with GCC and with Clang, and every case passed.

**Commit:** `a3166a3`, from a clean tree (branch `feat/m4.2-driver-classes`, PR #140).

**Bench:**
- **NUCLEO-F446RE** at 180 MHz. The console is USART2 through the ST-LINK's virtual COM port. The loopback is UART4, with a wire from A0 (PA0, TX) to A1 (PA1, RX) on the Arduino header.
- **Pico 2 W** at 150 MHz. The console is UART0, through the NUCLEO's relay. The loopback is UART1, with a wire from pin 6 (GPIO4, TX) to pin 7 (GPIO5, RX).

`ctest` on `cm4-nucleo-hil` and `cm33-pico2w-hil`, checked, with `-DEMB_TEST_UART_LOOP=uart4` and `=uart1`, each run's records under `hil-runs/`:

## What the runs show

- **Through the loopback wire** (`test_uart_loop`, through the class's API, on both boards):
  - a 64-byte echo written and read at once on the device's two lanes (A114);
  - bytes that arrived with no read waiting, kept in the ring and read later;
  - the hardware's idle line ending a read early: the STM32's IDLE flag and the PL011's receive timeout;
  - a kilobyte, every byte value, intact;
  - more bytes than the 64-byte ring holds, reported once as `EMB_EOVERRUN` with the 64 it kept;
  - a break arriving as a framing error that ends the waiting read. The STM32 sends its break frame with SBK. The PL011's break is timed by a software timer whose callback runs in its expiry interrupt.
- **On the console** (`test_uart_board`): a write by request sent by the driver's interrupts, a read timing out on a line nobody types on, a break, and a polled write.
- **The class's core** (`test_uart`): its fake loopback UART's seven cases, the same on the board as on the host.

## Found on the way

- **A floating receive pin.** The Pico's console receive pin, open on this bench, read as a continuous break, and every read failed with a framing error: the RP2350's pads come out of reset pulled down. The generated pin table now pulls a UART's receive pin up.
- **A too-short break.** The PL011's first break held BRK only while the UART reported busy, which a break does not set: a few microseconds, which the loopback never saw. It is now timed by a software timer.
