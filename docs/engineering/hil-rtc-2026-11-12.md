# The RTC class and the wall clock on hardware: NUCLEO-F446RE and Pico 2 W (2026-11-12)

The RTC class of SPEC-017 §10 and the wall clock of SPEC-003 §10 (A123). On each board, the RTC's time was set and read back, and kept against the kernel's clock over two seconds. Its alarm fired on time, and once canceled did not fire. Its time survived a reset and seeded the wall clock. The whole conformance suite ran on each board with GCC and with Clang, and every image passed. The same STM32 driver runs in CI on EmbSIM's NUCLEO-F446RE.

**Commit:** `aa704b2`, from a clean tree (branch `feat/m4.2-driver-classes`, PR #140). The class is `27e50d3`; `aa704b2` fixes the STM32 set's wait for its shift (below).

**Bench:** the wires of the UART, SPI and I2C reports. The RTC needs none. The clocks:
- **NUCLEO-F446RE:** the RTC in the backup domain on the board's 32.768 kHz LSE crystal (X2), its subseconds 1/256 s; no battery on VBAT, so the time is lost with power.
- **Pico 2 W:** the always-on timer in POWMAN, milliseconds on a 1 kHz tick from the 12 MHz crystal; no calendar, no backup registers.

`ctest` ran on `cm4-nucleo-hil` and `cm33-pico2w-hil`, checked, with the UART, SPI and I2C loops as before. Each run's records are under `hil-runs/`:

## What the runs show

**The board's RTC** (`test_rtc_board`):

| What | NUCLEO-F446RE (GCC / Clang) | Pico 2 W (GCC / Clang) |
|---|---|---|
| Set to 2026-11-10T08:00:00.5Z, then read: past the time set | 23 ms / 23 ms, of the 25 ms set and get took | 0 ms / 0 ms |
| Over the kernel's two seconds, the RTC moved | 2003 ms of 2005 / 2003 ms of 2005 | 2005 ms of 2006 / 2005 ms of 2005 |
| An alarm 300 ms ahead fired after, by the kernel's clock | 297 ms / 299 ms | 300 ms / 300 ms |
| After a reset, the RTC read past the time set | 27 ms / 27 ms | 4 ms / 4 ms |

- **Kept in step.** Two crystals agree within the RTC's resolution: 1/256 s on the STM32F4, 1 ms on the RP2350.
- **The alarm** fires within the RTC's resolution and the kernel's tick. A canceled one did not fire in the 400 ms after its time. One in the past was refused, and on the STM32F4 so was one past the month its alarm compares.
- **Across a reset.** Set, then reset by software (`EMB_ASSERT_RESETS`), the RTC kept counting. After the reset, the wall clock read what the RTC read, seeded when the RTC's device started.
- **Backup registers.** The STM32F4's twenty were written and read back. The RP2350's absence was reported as `EMB_ENOTSUP`.

**The class's core** (`test_rtc`) and **the wall clock** (`test_wallclock`) ran on the board as on the host:
- the RTC's time unset, then set;
- one alarm, armed again from its own callback, canceled, and disarmed by a set;
- the wall clock set, read and stepped by an hour with no deadline moving.

## Found on the way

- **A shift takes tens of milliseconds.** The STM32F4's set keeps the second's fraction by delaying the subseconds with the shift register. On the part, that shift completes in some 23 ms, longer than the driver's busy poll allowed, which ran out silently. The time was right throughout: the RTC holds the time set from the call on. But the set returned only when the shadow registers caught up. The set now waits for the shift in 1 ms sleeps, up to 100 ms, then copies the shadow registers again, as RM0390 asks. The board test bounds the first read by the time set and get took.
- **The alarm while a shift runs.** After a set's shift, the subsecond count runs past its prescaler for up to a second while the calendar shows the next second. An alarm converted from the time alone asked for a second the calendar never showed. Alarm A is now counted from the registers themselves, every subsecond bit compared (EmbSIM found it, `27e50d3`).
- **A loose wire.** The first NUCLEO runs failed `test_uart_loop` with both compilers: its A0 to A1 loop wire had come loose. Once it was reseated, both passed; the runs above are those.
