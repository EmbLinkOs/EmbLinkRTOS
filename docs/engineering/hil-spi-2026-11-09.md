# The SPI class on hardware: NUCLEO-F446RE and Pico 2 W (2026-11-09)

The SPI class of SPEC-017 §5, the transfer core, on both of M4's boards: the STM32 SPI driver on the NUCLEO and the PL022 on the Pico, each with its MOSI wired to its MISO. The whole conformance suite ran on each board with GCC and with Clang, and every image passed. The same PL022 source runs in CI under QEMU's MPS2 machines in the controller's own loopback mode, and the STM32 driver on EmbSIM's NUCLEO with the same wire.

**Commit:** `4b453a8`, from a clean tree (branch `feat/m4.2-driver-classes`, PR #140).

**Bench:**
- **NUCLEO-F446RE** at 180 MHz. SPI2 on APB1 (45 MHz), with a wire from CN10 pin 26 (PB15, MOSI) to pin 28 (PB14, MISO). The UART loopback, A0 to A1, stays fitted.
- **Pico 2 W** at 150 MHz. SPI1, with a wire from pin 15 (GPIO11, MOSI) to pin 16 (GPIO12, MISO). Its console goes through the NUCLEO's relay. The UART loopback, pin 6 to pin 7, stays fitted.

`ctest` on `cm4-nucleo-hil` and `cm33-pico2w-hil`, checked, with `-DEMB_TEST_SPI_LOOP=spi2` and `=spi1` (and the UART loopbacks as before), each run's records under `hil-runs/`:

## What the runs show

- **Through the loopback wire** (`test_spi_loop`, six cases, through the class's API on both boards):
  - 256 frames, every byte value, intact;
  - all four modes;
  - a slow clock (200 kHz) and a fast one (8 MHz), each the fastest divider not above the target's;
  - a 1 Hz target refused with `EMB_ENOTSUP`, and the controller still usable after;
  - ones sent when there is no transmit buffer;
  - a 256-frame transfer at 200 kHz stopped by a 2 ms timeout. It ended with `EMB_ETIMEDOUT` part-way, every frame it counted intact, and the next transfer clean. On both boards the case took that branch: the stop is real, not an emulator finishing first.
- **The class's core** (`test_spi`): its fake controller's five cases, the same on the boards as on the host.

## Found on the way

- **A wire one pin off.** The first NUCLEO run read zeros on every transfer. Over OpenOCD, driving each free pin of ports B and C in turn showed the wire joined PB1 (CN10 pin 24) to PB14. Moved to pin 26, the same scan showed PB15 to PB14, and the run passed.
- **A clock below the controller's slowest.** The first slow-clock case asked for 100 kHz. The F446's SPI2 reaches no lower than 45 MHz / 256 = 176 kHz, and the driver rightly refused. The case now asks for 200 kHz, and a new case checks the refusal.
- **A stop that cut a frame.** Both drivers first turned the controller off at once when a transfer was canceled, which on the STM32 clears SPE mid-frame, against RM0390. A canceled transfer now sends nothing more and completes from the interrupt that takes its last frame in flight.
- **Console lines cut by a reset.** In the Pico's Clang run, two `CASE` lines, one in `test_spi` and one in `test_uart`, arrived damaged: each was the line just before a case whose fault checks reset the board. The test framework resets with `emb_arch_system_reset()`, which did not wait for the PL011's 32-byte transmit FIFO, so the reset cut the line's tail. Both images report every test passed, but those two cases are missing from the run's case records. Fixed after this run: on Cortex-M, `emb_arch_system_reset()` now lets the board finish first (`emb_board_reset_prepare()`, as the Xtensa port has), and the RP2350 and STM32F4 boards flush their consoles there.

## Chip selects and the GPIO class (`0f776b5`)

The second step of the SPI class (A116): the GPIO class, each target's chip select, and the bus held across transfers. Every suite ran again on both boards, from a clean tree, with the same wires: the chip selects need none, since each board reads its own line back at the pin.

**Chip selects:** the NUCLEO's SPI2 has PB12 (CN10 pin 16), and the Pico's SPI1 has GPIO13 (pin 17). Each is in the board's description, and its pin table sets it up as an output, deasserted, at boot.

| Board | Compiler | Passed | Cases recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| NUCLEO-F446RE | Arm GNU Toolchain 14.2.Rel1 (GCC 14.2.1) | 34 of 34 | 225 |
| NUCLEO-F446RE | Homebrew Clang 23.1.5 with LLD 23.1.5 | 34 of 34 | 225 |
| Pico 2 W | Arm GNU Toolchain 14.2.Rel1 (GCC 14.2.1) | 35 of 35 | 227 |
| Pico 2 W | Homebrew Clang 23.1.5 with LLD 23.1.5 | 35 of 35 | 227 |

- **At the pin** (`test_spi_loop`'s new case, on both boards): the chip select high since boot; low while a 256-frame transfer runs, submitted without waiting; high again when it completes; low from `emb_drv_spi_acquire()` across two transfers, and high after `emb_drv_spi_release()`.
- **The GPIO class** (`test_gpio`): its fake controller's five cases, on the boards as on the host. The bus's hold is checked by `test_spi`'s fake: one chip select for a whole exchange, a held bus refusing another target, and a second thread waiting for it within its timeout.
- **No case line lost:** the Pico's GCC and Clang runs recorded the same 227 cases, now that a reset first lets the console finish (the defect above).

**On the way:** the first Pico run stopped when `picotool` lost its USB connection 30% into writing `test_spi_loop` (`picoboot::connection_error`), which left the board out of BOOTSEL and every later image unloaded. The 24 images before it had passed. The board was put back in BOOTSEL and both suites ran again from the start; only those runs are archived.
