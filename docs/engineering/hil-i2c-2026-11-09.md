# The I2C class on hardware: NUCLEO-F446RE and Pico 2 W (2026-11-09)

The I2C class of SPEC-017 §6, its first step: transactions of write and read segments, and target mode. On each board, two of the chip's own controllers are wired line to line: one makes transactions, the other answers them as a target. The whole conformance suite ran on each board with GCC and with Clang, and every image passed. The same STM32 driver runs in CI on EmbSIM's NUCLEO-F446RE, wired the same way.

**Commit:** `fe789d7`, from a clean tree (branch `feat/m4.2-driver-classes`, PR #140).

**Bench:** the lines' pull-ups are the chips' own, which the pin tables turn on; there are no resistors. The UART and SPI wires stay fitted.
- **NUCLEO-F446RE** at 180 MHz. I2C1 (controller) to I2C2 (target), APB1 at 45 MHz: SCL from D15 (PB8) to D6 (PB10), SDA from D14 (PB9) to D3 (PB3), on the Arduino header.
- **Pico 2 W** at 150 MHz. I2C0 (controller) to I2C1 (target), clocked by clk_sys: SCL from pin 12 (GPIO9) to pin 10 (GPIO7), SDA from pin 11 (GPIO8) to pin 9 (GPIO6).

`ctest` on `cm4-nucleo-hil` and `cm33-pico2w-hil`, checked, with `-DEMB_TEST_I2C_LOOP=i2c1,i2c2` and `=i2c0,i2c1` (and the UART and SPI loops as before), each run's records under `hil-runs/`:

## What the runs show

- **Between the two controllers** (`test_i2c_loop`, six cases, through the class's API):
  - six bytes written to the target's address, every value, received as sent;
  - register reads, a write of the register's address then a read after a repeated start, of one, two, three and eight bytes: the STM32 receiver's three acknowledge sequences, and DW_apb_i2c's command stream;
  - an address nobody answers, refused with `EMB_ENACK`, and the bus free for the next transaction;
  - bytes written beyond the target's buffer: refused by the STM32's target, which has `TARGET_NACK`, so the controller ends with `EMB_ENACK` after two; acknowledged and dropped by DW_apb_i2c's (A117); the target keeps the two that fit either way;
  - a register read at 400 kHz;
  - a listen no controller addresses, ending with `EMB_ETIMEDOUT`.
- **The class's core** (`test_i2c`): its fake controller's six cases, on the boards as on the host.

## Found on the way

- **A stale BTF.** EmbSIM's first runs read register values shifted by one byte, the register's own address first. A write segment's BTF was still set when the read segment after the repeated start began, and the receive path took it for a byte received. A receiver's BTF now counts only with RXNE.
- **A received byte sent back.** The STM32's target answered a register read with the address it had just received: it cleared the read's ADDR before taking that byte, and DR is also what a transmitter sends. It now takes a received byte first.
- **Interrupts that waited on the other controller.** A three-byte read left buffer interrupts on while waiting for BTF, which kept the controller's interrupt firing, and the target, on the same chip at the same priority, never ran to send the byte it waited for. Three-byte reads now turn them off from the start.
- **A target that cannot refuse mid-transaction.** On the Pico the controller expected the extra bytes refused and got all four acknowledged: DW_apb_i2c's `IC_SLV_DATA_NACK_ONLY` is written only while its target is idle. Rather than claim behaviour the hardware lacks, the class states it as a capability (A117).

## The held bus and recovery (`4f7b0a0`)

The class's second step: a bus held for one target across transactions, on the held bus the SPI class now shares (`<emb/drv_bus.h>`), and recovery of a bus a target holds low (A118). Every suite ran again on both boards, from a clean tree, with the same wires.

- **A stuck target freed** (`test_i2c_loop`'s new case, on both boards): the test, as a broken controller, clocks by hand a start, the target's address to read, its acknowledge and two bits of a byte of zeros, and stops. The target, the board's other controller, holds SDA low for its third bit; the case reads it low. `emb_drv_i2c_recover()` clocks it free, the target's listen ends, and a register read works after. On the Pico the class drives the lines open drain through the output enable, the RP2350's pads having none; the first try showed it, when its GPIO driver still refused open drain.
- **The held bus** (`test_i2c`, `test_spi`): the holder's transactions run, another target's are refused, other threads wait; recovery waits for a hold too.
